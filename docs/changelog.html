What’s new.

Maui isn’t out yet. This is what its first version brings.

1.0.0 — the first public release

Maui is the notch app that was called Island while it was being made. Everything kept on your Mac by earlier builds stays where it was.

  • Home — your newest screenshots fanned out in a row, a timer or a focus session, your next meeting, and the one thing waiting on you.
  • Screenshots — copy a screenshot's text, link or code; pick several with ⌘ or ⇧ and drag them out together; Clear forgets cards and keeps the files, unless you choose Move to Trash for screenshots (Maui asks, or you set it in Settings); files you dropped in are never trashed.
  • Drop anything on the notch — Keep a copy in the Tray, Send it to your other Mac (Reach), or Convert it (pictures, video, sound, and the words in PDFs and documents), saved beside the original.
  • Quick note — ⌃⌥N from any app opens one line under the notch; Return keeps it in the Tray, or, after Tab, sends it to a paired Mac nearby, whose notch says it and can answer with one line. Esc keeps what you typed for next time.
  • Read — a kept text opens as a teleprompter under the camera, kept out of captures made through macOS's own screen capture while it is read.
  • Apps — Claude Code, Codex, GitHub and Usage in one grid, with search. Each has its own page: before you connect, what connecting does; once connected, its own controls in the "…" beside the notch.
  • Coding agents — answer Claude Code's questions, plans and permission requests at the notch, including with a screenshot or a file; a question waits at the notch for up to 95 minutes, says how long it has waited, then goes back to the terminal. Codex's command approvals too (Codex asks for file access in its own window). Claude Code's and Codex's pages show each of your sessions as it works, in the agent's own words.
  • Focus — questions from other projects wait until a session ends.
  • Usage — how much of Claude's and Codex's limits is left, as vials that empty.
  • GitHub — your pull requests, their checks and reviews; Approve and Merge in one click each, verified against the exact commit.
  • Updates — Maui checks its update feed once a day; you can turn that off in Settings.
  • The look — the notch opens and closes as one shape, with its content travelling inside it; moving between pages slides and softens instead of cutting. Settings follows macOS's own new look.

Maui is free, and made with care by Abhinav.
